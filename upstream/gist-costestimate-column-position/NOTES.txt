Status: ready to file, NOT filed. Nothing has been sent to any list, posted anywhere, or entered in a commitfest. The
owner files it by hand.

1. What this is.
   - A pgsql-hackers discussion item and bug report, with no patch. gistcostestimate() costs a GiST scan by the
     selectivity of every qual, whichever index column the qual constrains.
     - So for a lookup on a column that a GiST index holds second, the planner prefers that index to a B-tree led by
       the column.
     - It then reads the whole GiST index: 892 of 892 pages against the B-tree's 2 at 100000 rows, and 8928 against 3
       at 1M rows.
     - The two plans are estimated 0.0125 apart at 100000 rows and 0.14 at 1M. The tie-break is the descent charge: ln
       against log2 comparisons, and log100(pages) against the B-tree's real height.
   - Known upstream since BUG #15408 (2018, pgsql-bugs). There Tom Lane confirmed that the estimate has no column
     modeling, and nobody took it further.
     - So email.txt is a reply to that thread, moved to -hackers, not a new report.
     - David Rowley's 2024 one-line log2 diff ([1]) and the open gist_trgm_ops thread, CF #7401 ([2]), are cited and
       not replied into. The first covers only the tie-break. The second has a different cause, a lossy
       single-column '=', and is converging on an opclass-level fix.
   - It targets PostgreSQL 20. It is a cost-model change, and Tom Lane said in 2018 that one would not be
     back-patched.

3. Why it matters to grizzle.
   - The index engine (packages/engine/src/physical/indexes/engine.ts) plans each request onto an index it claims
     serves it. The serving judge (test/pg/physical/serving.ts, judgeServing) runs every request's probe under
     EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS) and holds it to a buffer bound.
   - Some relations have requests that need conflicting key orders: a span predicate on (fixed, span), another on
     (span), and an equality lookup on (fixed).
     - Whichever column leads, one single-column probe is exposed.
     - PostgreSQL sweeps the GiST index that holds the probe's column outside its lead, in place of the index that
       serves it. repro.sql's sections 2 to 5 are those two orders in stock SQL.
   - The judge excuses exactly those sweeps.
     - serving.ts residueOf (line 994) names them "swept through another GiST index, gistcostestimate blind to its key
       order", when keyOrdersConflict (974) holds.
     - The request's own index alone must still serve the probe.
   - serving.ts's header (897 to 903) records the measurement at the suite's 20000 rows.
     - 6.50 through a GiST index on (span, numeric) of 144 pages, which it sweeps in 145 buffers.
     - 6.51 through a B-tree on (numeric, float8) of 97 pages, which reads 3.
     - The 0.01 gap is this report's 0.0125 = (ceil(log2 20000) - ceil(ln 20000)) * 0.0025 = (15 - 10) * 0.0025.
       Both indexes must then be costed at one page, since a page more would cost 4.0, and both at height 1, the
       GiST's (int) log100(144) and the 97-page B-tree's.
   - serving.test.ts keeps a case (line 1716), a join key beside span predicates whose GiST index PostgreSQL sweeps,
     that a drawn workload reaches.
   - TODO.md item 10's GiST bullet: "Closing it needs gistcostestimate to see column position, an upstream change."
     - This folder is that change's first step. Nothing in grizzle waits on it.
     - residueOf's GiST case goes, by hard cutover, once a release grizzle pins carries a fix. The kept case then
       becomes a served probe.
   - This research refines that bullet in two places (main's call whether to edit TODO.md):
     - A GiST-against-GiST tie needs no equal size.
       - Any two GiST indexes tie exactly when both pro-rata counts round to one page and both (int) log100(pages)
         agree.
       - 347 and 655 pages tie in repro.sql section 5, and the newest index wins (lcons and add_path).
     - The margin over a B-tree is not only ln against log2.
       - At 1M rows the 1204-page B-tree's real height is 2 (its cost admits no other), while the GiST height
         estimate, (int) log100(pages), stays 1 from 100 to 9999 pages. That adds 0.125 (2i).
       - So Rowley's log2 diff alone would not close the residue at 1M rows.
