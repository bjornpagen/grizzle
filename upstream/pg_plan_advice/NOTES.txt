NOTES: grizzle's side of the pg_plan_advice series (lane PGADVICE, 2026-11-27)

Why grizzle cares
- grizzle proves at compile time which index each keyed read uses and which columns it binds, and the carry-the-plan
  design (~/.cache/grizzle-release/status/CARRY-THE-PLAN.html) prints that proof both as SQL and as pg_plan_advice
  advice. Two of its workarounds exist only because of the gaps this series closes:
  - every keyed join is printed as a LATERAL subquery with LIMIT or OFFSET 0, which the planner never flattens,
    because advice could not carry the binding (gap a, finding 5 of CARRY-THE-PLAN). With PARAMETERIZED, a keyed
    join can be a plain join plus PARAMETERIZED(probed driver).
  - foreign keys into tables that writes delete from are checked by grizzle itself, at commit, instead of natively,
    because advice could not reach PostgreSQL's RI queries (gap c, FOREIGN-KEYS.html option 1). With qualified
    identifiers and cached-plan revalidation (gaps b and c), a transaction prologue can carry the RI checks' advice.
- None of this is a dependency: it reaches grizzle's hosts in PostgreSQL 20 at the earliest, and RDS later. Until
  then grizzle keeps its workarounds.

Decisions taken on the way (main relayed Bjorn's direction)
- No GUC to generate qualified identifiers: generated advice is exact for its statement; the qualifier is input
  syntax for whoever composes one string for many statements, which grizzle does.
- Cached-plan revalidation covers advisors too, with a generation fast path, so pg_stash_advice has no hole either.
- The core parameterization primitive is an interval (required, forbidden), not an exact set, so outer-join relids
  and lateral references need no special case in extensions.
