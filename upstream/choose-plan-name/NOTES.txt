NOTES: grizzle's side of choose_plan_name()'s O(N^3) naming of subplans

1. Where grizzle meets it (lane PLANSIZE, the owner's measurements, 2026-11-24).
   - grizzle's generated write statements carry hundreds of subplans: the largest, 2.28 MB, has 747 CTEs, 327 EXISTS
     sublinks, 99 ARRAY sublinks and 206 unnamed subqueries. PostgreSQL 19 names every one before planning it
     (8c49a484e8e), and choose_plan_name() makes the k-th exists_k, array_k or unnamed_subquery_k cost k scans of the
     list of every name so far.
   - In a sampled profile, choose_plan_name() was 40-65% of the planning CPU time of the large write statements.
     A build with the fix cut planning CPU by up to 2.9x (decideTest 2347 -> 813 ms), and left the reads with few
     sublinks as they were (EVIDENCE.txt 1).

2. Why grizzle removes the trigger itself, whatever upstream does.
   - Production is RDS, which runs only released PostgreSQL. A fix reaches it only as a 19.x minor release that RDS
     ships, and nothing has been committed yet (CHECKLIST.txt A). Robert Haas has said he would back-patch; that is
     not a commitment, and the minor release and RDS's uptake take months more.
   - So grizzle's own construction removes the trigger: lane INLINEPRINT prints single-use CTEs in place, so they
     take no name and plan no CTE subplan; lanes VALUECELL and STOREDERIVE4 cut the EXISTS and ARRAY sublinks, the
     numbered prefixes that cost the most (the k-th of a prefix tries k suffixes, each a scan of the whole list).
   - Once a release with the fix is what production runs, the naming costs grizzle nothing either way, and the
     remaining sublink and CTE counts matter only for what planning them costs otherwise (3).

3. What the fix leaves (found here, for PLANSIZE): planning many subplans is superlinear elsewhere too.
   - With choose_plan_name() constant time (ours or v6), planning SELECT ARRAY[EXISTS (SELECT 1), ...] still grows
     3 to 4.5 times per doubling of n (EVIDENCE.txt 2), and the planner's memory, the same in all three builds, from
     8 MB at n = 500 to 363 MB at n = 4000, about n^1.9: some per-subplan step is proportional to the number of
     subplans at that level. That is not choose_plan_name() and not this patch; it is the next bound to measure for
     statements with hundreds of sublinks, and another reason for VALUECELL and STOREDERIVE4 to cut them.

4. Where the commits were made.
   - A shallow clone of https://github.com/postgres/postgres.git at ~/.cache/grizzle/INLINEPRINT-pg, branches
     choose-plan-name (master 4931dab89d) and choose-plan-name-19 (REL_19_STABLE 4c1329da8f), with worktrees and
     meson builds beside it; all deleted when this package was finished. The patch files here are those commits'
     git format-patch: git am on a clean tree recreates them (README.txt 3).
   - The owner's measurement-only version, static state and not upstream quality, stays where it was:
     ~/.cache/grizzle/PLANSIZE-pg/choose_plan_name.patch, against the 19.0 tarball.
